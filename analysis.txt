==========================================
HOUSE PRICE PREDICTION - ANALYSIS
==========================================

💰 Sale Price Distribution

From the plot:
Most houses have sale prices concentrated toward the lower-to-middle price range.
There are fewer houses with very high sale prices.
The distribution is right-skewed, meaning the higher-priced houses extend further to the right.
There are some very expensive houses that may be considered potential outliers.
This suggests that SalePrice is not evenly distributed across the dataset.

"The distribution of SalePrice is right-skewed, with most houses 
concentrated between approximately R100,000 and R250,000. 
A smaller number of properties have substantially higher sale prices, 
resulting in a long right-hand tail."

⭐ Overall Quality vs Sale Price

From the plot:
Houses with lower Overall Quality scores generally have lower sale prices.
As Overall Quality increases, the sale price generally increases.
Houses with quality scores around 8–10 include many of the highest-priced properties.
Houses with the same quality score can still have different sale prices, showing that other features also influence price.
Overall, OverallQual appears to have a strong positive relationship with SalePrice.

The scatter plot indicates a positive relationship between Overall Quality 
and Sale Price. Higher-quality houses generally have higher sale prices, 
suggesting that Overall Quality is an important predictor of house prices.
However, the variation in sale prices at each quality level indicates that
 other housing characteristics also influence the final sale price.

🏠 Living Area vs Sale Price

From the plot:
Houses with smaller above-ground living areas generally have lower sale prices.
As living area increases, the sale price generally increases.
Houses with living areas above approximately 3,000 square feet include many of the higher-priced properties.
There are some houses with very large living areas but comparatively lower prices, which may indicate potential outliers.
Overall, GrLivArea shows a positive relationship with SalePrice, although other factors also affect the selling price.

The scatter plot shows a positive relationship between above-ground living
 area and house sale price. Generally, larger houses tend to have higher 
 sale prices. However, the variation in prices among houses with similar 
 living areas suggests that other factors also contribute to house prices.
  A few observations with unusually large living areas may also represent
   potential outliers.

🏗️ Year Built vs Sale Price

From the plot:
Older houses, especially those built around the late 1800s–early 1900s, are mostly at the lower end of the price range.
As the Year Built increases, the sale prices generally increase.
Houses built around the 1980s–2000s include many of the higher-priced properties.
There is still variation, meaning age alone doesn't determine the price.

The scatter plot indicates a positive relationship between the year a 
house was built and its sale price. Generally, newer houses tend to have 
higher sale prices than older houses. However, considerable variation 
exists, indicating that other factors also contribute to house prices.
